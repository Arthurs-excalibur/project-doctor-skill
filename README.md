# 🩺 Project Doctor

### Built something with AI? Let's get it ready to ship.

You spent hours prompting, tweaking, debugging, and vibe coding your dream app. It finally works on your machine. Now you want to deploy it.

But wait...

**Is it actually ready for production? And where the heck can you host it for free?**

That's where **Project Doctor** comes in.

Project Doctor is an AI-powered software maintenance skill that examines your project, cleans up unnecessary code, fixes bugs, checks whether things actually work, and helps you figure out how to deploy your app — **without spending money you don't have.**

Think of it as a pre-flight check for your AI-built projects. Before you ship, let it take a look under the hood.

## 🩺 What can Project Doctor do for you?

### 🔍 1. Check your project before you ship

Your app might run locally, but that doesn't automatically mean it's ready for real users.

Project Doctor examines your codebase to uncover potential problems, from broken configurations and outdated dependencies to security risks, missing tests, and questionable architecture.

It helps you understand what's working, what's broken, and what deserves attention before deployment.

### 🧹 2. Clean up the AI-generated spaghetti

Let's be honest. Vibe coding can leave behind unused files, redundant packages, duplicated logic, and three different implementations of the same feature.

Project Doctor investigates your project, identifies unnecessary complexity, and refactors or removes what it can safely justify removing.

**Less clutter. Less technical debt. A project you can actually maintain.**

### 🛠️ 3. Fix bugs instead of just listing them

Finding problems is nice. Fixing them is better.

Project Doctor can investigate errors, repair broken implementations, improve error handling, and work through issues introduced during refactoring.

It doesn't just aim to make the code look cleaner — it aims to make the project work better.

### 🧪 4. Test whether your fixes actually work

"Looks good to me" isn't a testing strategy.

Project Doctor runs the checks your project supports, such as tests, type checks, linting, and production builds. When useful, it can add regression tests to help prevent old bugs from coming back.

It reports what passed, what failed, and what still needs manual verification.

Because a successful build doesn't necessarily mean your app is bug-free.

### ☁️ 5. Find a way to deploy it for FREE

**Here's the best part.** Project Doctor doesn't stop at telling you to deploy your app. It helps you figure out *how*.

It analyzes your application's actual requirements and researches suitable deployment options, prioritizing free hosting and services that fit your budget.

It can help you determine:

- **Where to host:** Find suitable platforms for your frontend, backend, APIs, and other components.
- **What infrastructure you need:** Figure out whether your app requires a database, persistent server, background worker, or just static hosting.
- **What it will cost:** Understand free-tier quotas, storage limits, bandwidth restrictions, and potential charges before committing.
- **How to deploy:** Get a practical plan with the relevant build commands, output directories, configuration, and environment variables.
- **What to watch out for:** Avoid deployment choices that look free at first but don't fit your app's needs.

No blindly recommending the same hosting provider to every project. No assuming your full-stack app can run on static hosting.

**The goal? Get your project into the hands of real users without unnecessarily reaching for your wallet.**

## ⚡ How it works

**01 — Inspect**

Point Project Doctor at your project. It investigates the codebase, architecture, dependencies, configuration, and available tests.

**02 — Diagnose**

It prioritizes the problems that matter, from broken functionality and security concerns to unnecessary complexity and deployment blockers.

**03 — Repair**

It cleans up code, refactors implementations, fixes defects, and adds tests where appropriate — within defined safety boundaries.

**04 — Verify**

It reruns relevant checks, investigates regressions, and reports what it could and couldn't verify.

**05 — Plan your launch**

It researches suitable hosting options, prioritizes genuinely free solutions, and gives you a deployment plan tailored to your application.

---

## 🛡️ Autonomous, but not reckless

Project Doctor is designed to do more than generate a checklist. It can investigate issues, make justified changes, run checks, and iterate on repairs.

But your project is still your project.

It should protect existing work, avoid exposing secrets, and stop before actions that could irreversibly destroy data, rewrite Git history, incur unexpected costs, or publish changes without authorization.

It also won't pretend that every issue is fixed just because a build succeeds.
